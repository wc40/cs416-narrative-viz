What actually predicts how much energy a person uses?

Energy consumption per person varies more than a thousandfold across the world. Three plausible explanations — how many people a country has, how big its economy is, and how much its people earn — turn out to be wildly unequal in explanatory power.

Both axes are logarithmic. Circle area is proportional to the share of energy from renewable sources.

Reading this chart honestly. These are cross-sectional correlations for a single year, not causal estimates. Rich countries can afford more energy, and abundant energy helps economies grow; this data cannot separate the two directions. Renewable share is reported for only 64 of 153 countries, and those 64 skew wealthy — so any pattern involving circle size is drawn from a non-random subset and is shown for context rather than as a finding.

Data: Our World in Data — Energy (consumption, renewables share) and World Bank World Development Indicators (population, GDP, adjusted net national income per capita). Reference year 2020. Full method notes in the README.
